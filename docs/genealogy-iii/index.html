The record

  • 261 individuals across 7 generation columns; 72 marriages; 192 parent–child links.
  • Transcribed from the plate and verified against it. Clan descent is matrilineal, which gives every bracket an independent check; all 72 marriages pass it.

Navigating this chart

  • A + at the start of a line marks a spouse, printed on the line below the person they married.
  • F. and M. give the sex of each person as printed.
  • The horizontal leader rule runs from a person to the bracket holding their children; the bracket itself hangs on the mother’s line.
  • A number after a name is the person’s age when the data was collected. d. means they had already died when Parsons recorded the genealogy, during her fieldwork of 1918–19; where she knew the year she gives it, as in d. 1913.
  • Every person number is a stable link — #p1 addresses person 1’s first printed line, and the address bar keeps it while you read.
  • The chart region can be focused and panned with the arrow keys; on a narrow screen, scroll sideways to follow the later generations.
  • With JavaScript: with the chart focused, press / to find a person by number or name; selecting a person number opens its register card, and Esc closes it. The Theme and Scale controls remember your choice.

Editorial notes
  • Clan membership is matrilineal: every sibling group carries its mother's clan. That rule was used to verify the bracket readings against the plate.
  • A dash in place of a name means the plate printed no name.
  • The dashed rule separating the two halves is an editorial addition, not on the plate. It marks the two founding couples, 1+2 and 230+231.
  • This plate says which marriage a sibling group belongs to, where Genealogies I and II leave it open. The rule reaching a bracket is drawn from the line of the parent whose marriage it is, so where someone has two spouses, each spouse’s line either carries that rule or does not. 86 and 87 are the case that would need an editorial reading on another plate — one woman, two husbands, one bracket — and here it needs none: 86’s line carries the rule and 87’s does not. Nothing on this table is attributed by this edition; the plate does it.
  • One bracket on the plate is drawn further than it should be. 22’s children are 80 and 82, and 83 is 25’s son — 25’s own rule reaches him on his row. But the vertical that gathers 80 and 82 is carried on down past 82 to touch 83 as well, so on the plate the three appear to share one bracket. The chart draws two, which is the one place on this table where it departs from the scan. All three children are of the Corn clan, as both mothers are, so matrilineal descent cannot settle it; the two rules entering the bracket, on 22’s row and 25’s, are the evidence. Elsewhere this plate keeps adjacent brackets plainly apart, offsetting their verticals where they meet.
  • Four couples are printed twice, the second setting carrying the plate’s own “For her descendants, see above” in place of the bracket: 7+8, 91+92, 124+125 and 152+153. Each is drawn once here, where its issue is drawn. 166 and 167 are printed twice as well, with no descendants line. Person 8 is what joins the plate’s two blocks: husband of 7 in the first and a son of 236+237 in the second.
  • The numbering on this plate is not a unique key. The numbers 258 and 259 are each printed on two different people, and 256 and 257 appear nowhere. Both pairs were read at high magnification to rule out a misreading. This edition states the fact and does not guess at the cause: all four rows print the number the plate prints, and each is a separate entry in the register with its own card.
  • Three entries are printed with a value this edition reads as a misprint, and each is set on the chart as the plate sets it, ringed in red with this note beneath it. The chart is not corrected; the correction is here.
    • 37 is printed M. She is the mother of 109-112, whose clan is Chaparral Cock, where her husband 36 is Lizard. Clan descent is matrilineal, so the children’s clan is hers and she is a woman; her bracket and her name agree. Read at high magnification to rule out a broken F. Everything computed from this table — her bracket, her children’s clan, the register — follows the reading, not the letter.
    • 50’s clan is printed Chapparral Cock, with a doubled p. Her own four children 135-138, and every other line on this plate, print Chaparral Cock.
    • 255’s clan is printed Bager for Badger.
  • This plate’s cross-references into Genealogies I and II have been checked against this edition’s transcriptions of those plates, by name, sex and clan — fifty-one references on the numbered lines and five more in the two prose notes under 155. They are reproduced exactly as printed, and the numbers are deliberately not links: another plate’s numbering is not an anchor on this page, so following one means opening that table and looking the number up there. This plate does not share Genealogy II’s displacement. Table 2’s references into Genealogy I run one too high from its person 66 onward; this plate’s are exact right across that same range, its citations of Genealogy I’s 78, 79, 97, 98, 99, 100, 101, 103 and 104 each reaching the person finally printed under that number. Genealogy III was numbered against the Genealogy I that went to press; Genealogy II was not. Neither plate’s numbering may be used to correct the other. Four references do not resolve as printed, and no two are the same kind of slip:
    • 170-174 cite Genealogy II’s 191 to 195 on five consecutive lines. The people named there are its 201 to 205 — five for five, in that order, all of the Sun clan. Genealogy II’s actual 191 to 195 are a group of Oak people, and this plate cites them correctly elsewhere, from 194, 195 and 198.
    • 218 cites Genealogy I’s 101, who is the father already cited from 257. The person meant is its 102. Her sisters 219 and 261 cite 103 and 104 exactly, so this is an isolated slip and not a run.
    • 173 cites “Gen. I, 149”, and Genealogy I numbers 104 people. The reference resolves to nobody. It was read again at high magnification: 149 is what the plate prints. The person is Genealogy I’s 49, which is how Genealogy II cites her from its own 204.
    • The prose note under 155 sends a reader to “Gen. I, 8, 90”. The husband, 8, is exact; the descendant is 89, the one child of Genealogy I’s 73+8. This is the only place on this plate where Genealogy II’s displacement appears, and it attests it from a third plate. 155’s other prose note, into Genealogy II, resolves exactly.
  • Many entries carry no sex letter — most of the unnamed children in the last two generations, and 155, 193, 195 and 260, who are named or numbered but printed without one. Where the plate records no sex, none is supplied.
  • The generation ruler above the chart, the linked person numbers, and the register below the chart are editorial apparatus of this edition, not part of the plate.
